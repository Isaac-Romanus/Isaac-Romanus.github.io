## Definition

**Follicular lymphoma (FL)** is an indolent B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma of germinal
center origin, composed of centrocytes and centroblasts arranged in a follicular
pattern.

## Epidemiology

Second most common non-Hodgkin lymphoma in the West; median age around 60.

## Clinical features

Painless, often widespread lymphadenopathy; frequently disseminated (including marrow)
at diagnosis despite an indolent course.

## Microscopic (histology)

- **Effaced** nodal architecture by crowded, **back-to-back follicles**.
- Follicles are monotonous, **lack tingible-body macrophages**, and lose normal
  polarization.
- Mixture of small cleaved **centrocytes** and larger **centroblasts**; grading is by
  centroblast count per high-power field.

## Immunohistochemistry & special stains

Neoplastic follicles are **CD20+, CD10+, BCL6+**, and characteristically **BCL2+**
(normal germinal centers are BCL2&minus;). CD21 highlights follicular dendritic meshworks.

## Molecular

**t(14;18)(q32;q21)** juxtaposes **BCL2** to the **IGH** locus, causing BCL2
overexpression and apoptosis resistance in ~85% of cases.

## Differential diagnosis

- Reactive follicular hyperplasia (BCL2&minus; germinal centers, preserved polarity)
- Mantle cell lymphoma (CD5+, cyclin D1+)
- Marginal zone lymphoma

## Prognosis & treatment

Indolent but generally incurable; grade and stage guide management. Watch for
transformation to diffuse large B-cell lymphoma.
