## Definition

A **tubular adenoma** is a benign, dysplastic epithelial neoplasm of the colon composed
predominantly (> 75%) of tubular glands. It is the most common colorectal polyp and a
precursor in the conventional adenoma&ndash;carcinoma sequence.

## Epidemiology

Very common, increasing with age; most patients are over 50. Frequently detected at
screening colonoscopy.

## Clinical features

Usually asymptomatic and found incidentally. Larger lesions may cause occult bleeding.

## Gross

Small, often pedunculated polyp with a smooth or slightly lobulated surface.

## Microscopic (histology)

- Crowded, elongated **tubular** glands lined by dysplastic epithelium.
- **Low-grade dysplasia:** elongated, hyperchromatic, pseudostratified ("pencillate")
  nuclei confined to the basal half, with preserved polarity.
- Reduced goblet cells; increased mitoses.
- By definition, at least low-grade dysplasia is present.

## Immunohistochemistry & special stains

Generally not required. Ki-67 shows expansion of the proliferative zone; p53 is usually
wild-type in low-grade lesions.

## Molecular

Early **APC** loss (Wnt pathway) is characteristic, with later **KRAS** and **TP53**
alterations as lesions progress.

## Differential diagnosis

- Reactive/regenerative epithelium
- Sessile serrated lesion
- Villous or tubulovillous adenoma (villous component > 25%)

## Prognosis & treatment

Excellent after complete polypectomy. Surveillance interval depends on size, number,
villous architecture, and grade of dysplasia.
