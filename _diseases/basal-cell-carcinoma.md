## Definition

**Basal cell carcinoma (BCC)** is the most common human malignancy: a locally invasive
tumor of basaloid keratinocytes that only rarely metastasizes.

{% include callout.html kind="pearl" text="Peripheral palisading + stromal clefting is the classic low-power clue — confirm at high power before calling a basaloid mimic." %}

## Epidemiology

Older, fair-skinned individuals with chronic UV exposure. Increasing incidence.

## Clinical features

Pearly papule with telangiectasias, often with a rolled border and central ulceration
("rodent ulcer") on the face.

## Gross

Pearly, sometimes ulcerated nodule or plaque on sun-exposed skin.

## Microscopic (histology)

- Nests of **basaloid** cells with scant cytoplasm and hyperchromatic nuclei.
- **Peripheral palisading** of nuclei around the nests.
- **Retraction (clefting)** artifact between tumor nests and surrounding myxoid stroma.
- Frequent mitoses and apoptotic bodies; connection to the epidermis is common.

## Immunohistochemistry & special stains

Usually a morphologic diagnosis. **BerEP4** positive; **EMA** negative (helps separate
from squamous cell carcinoma).

## Molecular

Dysregulated **Hedgehog** signaling, commonly via **PTCH1** loss or **SMO** mutation.

## Differential diagnosis

- Basaloid squamous cell carcinoma
- Trichoepithelioma / trichoblastoma
- Sebaceous carcinoma

## Prognosis & treatment

Excellent with complete excision. Infiltrative, morpheaform, and micronodular subtypes
are more aggressive; Mohs surgery is used for high-risk sites.
